# Kenwood pathway: North vs. Southwest (research notes, Oct. 8, 2026)

Prepared in response to a reviewer's concern: "your plan keeps trying to send kids from Kenwood to North instead of Southwest... according to the NYT only 13 families actually stuck with the MPS's plan and they were all gone before the end of the year."

## What the 2021 redesign did
- The Comprehensive District Design (approved May 12, 2020; in effect from 2021-22) moved Kenwood Elementary's pathway from Southwest High to Anwatin Middle and North High.
  - Southwest Voices: https://www.southwestvoices.news/posts/what-is-the-cdd-and-how-has-it-impacted-mps-families
- Kenwood serves Lowry Hill. Star Tribune (Mar. 6, 2020) projected Southwest "from 1,833 down to 1,249" after losing "a large swath of its attendance zone to North High."
  - https://www.startribune.com/minneapolis-school-board-hears-latest-on-high-school-boundary-changes/568543612

## The NYT claim: partly supported
- **Source:** Sarah Mervosh, "In Minneapolis Schools, White Families Are Asked to Help Do the Integrating," NYT, Nov. 2021. Read through syndicated copies and summaries:
  - https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-mercury-news/20211128/281694028053502
  - https://www.ebony.com/white-students-are-integrating-a-black-high-school-in-minneapolis/
  - https://www.minnpost.com/glean/2021/11/new-york-times-report-details-changes-at-north-high-after-district-redesign-plan/
- **What it reports for fall 2021:** North had 440 students, 30 of them white. Per the Ebony summary, 13 white students were in the freshman class, the first class under the new boundaries.
- **The "13" is 13 white ninth-graders,** not "13 families who stuck with the plan."
- **"All gone before the end of the year" is not supported.** No source for it was found.
- **The spirit of the claim is supported.** Star Tribune, Oct. 18, 2025: North's principal estimated that over five years the south-side boundary change brought about 15 white students and 75-80 Black and Latino students. Parents described few Kenwood classmates at Anwatin, and families moving to St. Louis Park or choosing Southwest anyway.
  - https://www.startribune.com/five-years-after-controversial-plan-minneapolis-schools-confront-same-equity-and-budget-woes/601475496
- **Recommended wording for the deck:** "Few Kenwood-area families followed the 2021 move to North. North's principal estimates about 15 white students came from the new south-side zones in five years (Star Tribune, Oct. 2025)."

## The district's own 2026 options
Source: Ward 7 News, Sept. 18, 2026 (https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MPLS/bulletins/42b6f29); North News, Sept. 27, 2026; Minnesota Reformer, Sept. 16, 2026.
- **Options 2 and 3:** Kenwood-area students go through Anthony Middle to Southwest High.
- **Option 1:** Kenwood goes to Franklin Middle and North High.
- **All options:** Anwatin closes. North Bryn Mawr goes Bryn Mawr, then Franklin, then North.

## Feasibility of Kenwood to Southwest
- **Southwest High:** 1,170 students (workbook SY26), design enrollment 1,959-2,177, so 790-1,000 open seats. MPS building report (Oct. 1, 2024): 1,198 of about 2,030.
- **Kenwood's high school cohort:** Kenwood K-5 has 370 students, about 62 per grade. Even with every one continuing, that is about 250 high schoolers, a quarter to a third of Southwest's open seats.
- **Distance from Kenwood** (straight line, from approximate coordinates): Southwest about 3.1 mi, North about 2.5 mi. MPS high schoolers ride Metro Transit with a student pass, so no yellow-bus routes are added.
- **Middle school: the hard part.**
  - Anwatin is 1.1 road mi from Kenwood. Anthony is about 5.7 road mi.
  - Anthony has 765 students against a design of 866-963 (about 100-200 seats), so Kenwood's roughly 150-185 middle schoolers would nearly fill it.
  - In the model, the district's route through Anthony (Options 2 and 3) adds about $1.3M a year in bussing and costs about $2.1-2.2M a year in net savings, compared with sending all of Anwatin to Franklin.
