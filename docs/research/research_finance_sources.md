### FY27 budget gap: how the figure moved
- Oct. 2025 five-year pro forma: about $30M. Feb. 10, 2026 board presentation: **about $50.5M**. https://alphanews.org/minneapolis-public-schools-projects-more-than-50-million-budget-deficit-for-next-school-year/ (MED). The fringe rate for FY27 is 38.55% districtwide.
- May 2026: recovered special-education aid added +$10.7M (FY26) and +$10.8M (FY27), which brought the gap to **$39.7M**. https://fox9.com/news/minneapolis-schools-find-new-funding-budget-gap-shrinks-may-2026 (HIGH, matches KSTP and CBS)
- The $39.7M gap was closed in June with a balanced budget and no one-time funds. https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minneapolis-public-schools-proposed-closures-funding/ (HIGH)
- Fox9 video caption (May 2026): a shortfall "in the years ahead around $100 million." No year is given. (LOW to MED)

### FY28 and later projections
- **FY28 gap: no published figure found.** The Oct. 2024 pro forma said deficits would grow to about $100M within four years, and FY26 was then projected at $84M. https://minnesotareformer.com/2024/10/17/minneapolis-public-schools-latest-financial-projections-again-show-eminent-budget-crisis-without-action/ (MED, dated)
- **ESTIMATE, FY28 structural gap of $25–45M.** Reasoning: FY27 was balanced with recurring cuts. The underlying drivers continue: about 3–4% compensation growth on a roughly $580M staff base (about 80% of the general fund) against about 2–3% formula growth. That leaves roughly $10–20M of new annual pressure, plus the end of any one-time items in FY27. MPS has also re-forecast each year's gap upward mid-cycle by about $20M (FY27 went from $30M to $50M). Treat the upper end as plausible.

### FY2026 (2025-26)
- The operating deficit was $95.1M before the $20M tech levy (net about $75M). Fox9, June 2025: https://www.fox9.com/news/minneapolis-schools-layoffs-budget-shortfall-june-2025 (MED). After cuts, 5,634 FTE remain. The district has 73 buildings on 436 acres, and 15 are more than 100 years old.
- **The FY26 adopted general fund total was not found.** ESTIMATE: about $720–740M, similar to FY27's $734.7M general fund expenditure.

### Reserves and fund balance
- General fund balance was **$112M** at the start of FY26, against the $140M previously reported. Board policy requires reserves of at least 8% of operating expenses. MPS is out of compliance on unassigned fund balance and is moving about $3.6M to reserves. Minnesota Reformer, May 1, 2026: https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/05/01/a-year-of-chaos-and-dysfunction-inside-the-finance-department-of-minneapolis-public-schools/ (MED)
- Reserves used in earlier years: FY25 used $55M of assigned/one-time reserves (Sahan Journal FAQ). https://sahanjournal.com/education/minneapolis-public-schools-budget-cuts-faq/ (MED)
- Federal pandemic aid totaled about $259–264M from 2020 to 2024, now exhausted. (MED)

  - **Option 1: about $721,000**
  - **Option 2: about $184,000**
  - **Option 3: about $2.36M**
- General fund cost of affected operations: about **$217.5M** today, against **$218.9–220.9M** after the plan. Gross reductions in utilities and transportation are reinvested into counselors, behavior support, intervention, arts, and similar positions. Net savings are **$0.72–2.3M**. CBS: https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minneapolis-public-schools-proposed-closures-funding/ (HIGH)
- **Utility savings: $560,000–705,000 per year.** This brackets the deck's $635k for Option 1. Busing savings are expected from start-time changes, but no dollar figure was given. https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/school-closures-big-questions-how-much-could-minneapolis-public-schools-actually-save/ (MED)
- **The district's own attrition assumption is about 200 students leaving MPS** (Deputy Supt. Ty Thompson). (HIGH, CBS and KSTP)
- Superintendent Sayles-Adams: "We expect to essentially spend the same amount of money." Fox9: https://www.fox9.com/news/proposed-minneapolis-school-closures-district-share-details-sept-25-2026 (HIGH)
- Enrollment is about 29,000 against capacity of about 42,000, leaving about 13,000 empty seats. About two-thirds of schools are currently below full-funding enrollment; under the options, about one-third would be.
- Minimum enrollment thresholds (set Jan. 2026), from Minnesota Reformer: https://minnesotareformer.com/?p=38712 (HIGH)
  - Elementary: 360–477
  - Middle: 594–756
  - High: 888–1,064
- Earlier, larger savings claims, for context:
  - The 2023 pro forma estimated about **$45M/yr** from "right-sizing" the footprint. https://www.mplsschoolsvoices.news/posts/half-of-minneapolis-public-schools-buildings-are-under-utilized (LOW to MED)
  - A 2024 Sahan Journal FAQ cites "closing 22 schools would save $23M annually" (about $1.05M per school). (LOW, source of the estimate unclear)
- **Modeling implication:** 200 departing students × about $12k marginal revenue (Section 2) is about $2.4M per year in lost revenue. That is larger than the Option 1 net savings ($0.72M) and the Option 2 net savings ($0.18M), and roughly equal to Option 3 ($2.36M).

**Derived figures (ESTIMATE):**
- General fund revenue per enrolled student: $737.25M ÷ 29,149 = **about $25,300**. General fund spending per student is about $25,200.
- Implied APU: $249.15M ÷ $7,683 = **about 32,430 APU**.
- MPS referendum per pupil: $69.36M ÷ about 32,400 is **about $2,140 per pupil unit**.
  - Documented history: the operating levy was $1,604 per pupil before 2018 (MinnPost 2018). Nov. 2018 voters added **$490 per pupil, inflation-indexed, for 7 years**, for a total of about $2,094 in 2018 dollars.
  - Sources: https://www.minnpost.com/education/2018/08/minneapolis-public-schools-will-be-asking-voters-support-30-million-referendum-nov/ and https://naomikritzer.com/2018/10/15/election-2018-school-funding/
  - I could not confirm the current authorized per-pupil amount or the renewal date. Use about $2,100–2,300 (MED).
- Average compensatory per enrolled student: $53.66M ÷ 29,149 is about $1,840. Per eligible student (about 60–65% FRL), it is about **$2,800–3,100**.

**Marginal revenue lost when one student leaves (ESTIMATE), per year, steady state:**
- Typical K–6 general-education, FRL-eligible student: basic $7,683 + referendum about $2,140 + compensatory about $2,900 (lagged one year) + EL about $700 if applicable = **about $12,000–13,400**.
- Non-FRL student: about $9,800.
- Grades 7–12: add 20% to basic and referendum.
- Special-education students add aid tied to their services, but cost falls with it, so the net effect is roughly neutral or a loss.
- The "Other general levy" ($54M) is largely fixed authority and not lost per student.
- Minnesota declining-enrollment revenue cushions about 28% of the formula on the first-year loss.
- City of Minneapolis Auditor rule of thumb: "about $10,000 revenue per student." https://www.minneapolismn.gov/media/-www-content-assets/documents/Public-School-Enrollment-Incentives.pdf (MED)
- **Recommended model input: $11,500 per departing student per year, with a range of $10,000–13,500.**

## 5. Building operating costs and capital

- **Deck figure:** Option 1 saves $635k per year in utilities from about 1.0M operating sq ft, which works out to **about $0.64 per sq ft**. The district range across options is $560–705k per year (KSTP, above).
- **Benchmarks:**
  - The national energy benchmark is about $1.00 per sq ft per year (AS&U, about 2001), with a range of $0.90–1.16. Texas LBB: https://www.lbb.texas.gov/Documents/Publications/Primer/Energy_Efficient_Education.pdf (LOW, dated)
  - US K-12 energy spending is about $8B per year, the second-largest cost after salaries. DOE: https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/sectors/k-12-school-districts
  - ESTIMATE: in 2026 dollars, typical Upper-Midwest K-12 utilities run **$1.00–1.60 per sq ft**. MPS's $0.64 per sq ft is plausible only if mothballed buildings stay heated, keep some utilities on, or are re-used. Treat it as net savings, not gross.
- **Custodial and maintenance, ESTIMATE:** custodial about $3–5 per sq ft and routine maintenance about $1–2 per sq ft (2026 dollars). Total operations and maintenance excluding capital is about **$5–8 per sq ft**. Most of the custodial portion is avoidable only if positions are actually eliminated.
- **MPS capital plan, FY27–29** (https://budget.mpschools.org/Capital_Plan_Budget, HIGH):

## 6. Enrollment loss after closures, and MPS enrollment trends

### Research evidence

**Synthesis (ESTIMATE):**
- Direct exit among displaced students is about **5–10% above baseline attrition** in districts with low choice (Chicago, Texas). Minneapolis has very high choice: about 42% of resident students are already in charters or open-enrolled out. The CDD evidence suggests disruption-driven exit there can far exceed the district's 200-student assumption.
- About 3,500–4,000 students are displaced (12–14% of 29,000).
  - **Low case:** 5% leave, about 200 students (the district's assumption).
  - **Central case:** 8–12% leave, about 300–450 students.
  - **High case:** 15–20% leave, plus spillover of about 1% systemwide (about 290 students), for **about 800–1,000 students**.
- At about $11.5k each:
  - Central case: **about $3.5–5M per year**
  - High case: **about $9–11.5M per year**
  - Low case: $2.3M
- For spillover, the CDD analog shows disruption-year enrollment declining about 4–6 percentage points faster than forecast.

## 7. Itinerant or shared specialists

- **No rigorous US cost study was found** on travel time for itinerant art, music, or PE teachers. Milwaukee PS uses "Traveling Music Teachers" allocated by hours: 400 hours for a K-8, 70 for a middle school, 250 for a high school. No travel-time data: https://milwaukeepublic.ic-board.com/attachments/15193071-a33d-4a9d-b64f-050acd044e0e.pdf (LOW relevance)
- MPS plan context: the proposal guarantees a **full-time** art, music, and PE teacher at every elementary school (Minnesota Reformer, Sept. 2026). In today's small schools, specialists are often fractional, since the staffing model allocates 0.2 FTE specialist per classroom.
- IRS 2026 mileage rate: **$0.725 per mile**. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-sets-2026-business-standard-mileage-rate-at-725-cents-per-mile-up-25-cents (HIGH)
- **ESTIMATE, cost of splitting a specialist across two buildings:**
  - One midday transfer takes 15–25 minutes of driving plus 10–20 minutes of packing, setup, and parking, so about 30–45 minutes per day. That is about 7–10% of a 7.25-hour duty day, or **about 0.07–0.10 FTE lost, about $9–12k per shared position per year** at $123,762.
  - Mileage: about 8–15 miles per day × 175 days × $0.725 is about **$1,000–1,900 per year**.
  - **Total about $10–14k per shared specialist per year**, plus harder-to-price effects: less collaboration, no duty coverage, and lower retention.
  - Example: a small school needing 0.5 FTE each of art, music, and PE pays for the rest of each FTE in idle or "flex" time if staffed full-time. Sharing saves about 1.5 × $124k = $186k gross, against about $30–40k in travel cost and lost time.
